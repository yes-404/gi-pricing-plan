## Why `mtime` and not `written_at` — the measurement is the finding

**A first pass of this observation reported the file as "stale", which is the weaker claim
and the wrong instrument.** `written_at` cannot separate two states that matter differently:

| State | `written_at` | `mtime` |
|---|---|---|
| the cycle ran and re-derived an unchanged position | old | **recent** |
| the cycle has not run at all | old | **old** |

A field that reads identically in both cases cannot distinguish them — `NT-0007`'s shape
exactly, *"a boundary metric reads zero by construction … a heavy session and a disciplined
one produce the same zero."* **`mtime` distinguishes them**, and it is the instrument
`NT-0014`'s own reasoning points at.

The stronger claim is therefore the evidenced one: **the writer has not run**, not merely
that its content is old.

## What this finding deliberately does not claim

`mtime` proves absence of writes. It does not identify the cause, and three remain open:

1. the watcher is alive and not writing;
2. the watcher is dead;
3. the watcher was never re-armed this session.

**No attempt was made to narrow this by reading the log or by `pgrep`.** Both answer a
different question than the one asked — process existence, not write behaviour — and
`NT-0014`'s own reasoning is that an anomaly-only monitor's silence is indistinguishable
from its death. A liveness proof would launder a staleness defect rather than resolve it.
Narrowing it is the owner's first step, not this record's.

## Falsifiable

Discharged when `mtime` advances on a cycle that **re-derives** `position` from
`docs/roadmap.md` — proven by **two consecutive cycles** moving it, not by one write, which
a manual `show` invocation can produce and which would therefore prove the writer is
reachable rather than running. Re-opened if `mtime` stops advancing for longer than the
watcher's own cycle interval while a session is live.
